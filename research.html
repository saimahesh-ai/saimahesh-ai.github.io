Current Projects

Byzantine-Robust Architectures for Edge Deployments

Problem: Federated learning systems in agricultural edge networks are vulnerable to Byzantine faults — malicious or faulty nodes can corrupt the global model, especially when communication bandwidth is limited.

Solution: I designed DPRS, a Byzantine-robust federated learning framework that combines INT4/INT8 mixed-precision quantization with a Temporal Checkpoint Manifold for automated failover. This reduces bandwidth by 60% while sustaining 98.04% accuracy, making secure federated learning practical on resource-constrained agricultural edge devices.

Parameter-Free Dynamic Routing Networks

Problem: Existing lightweight models for crop disease classification sacrifice spatial explainability for compactness, making their predictions untrustworthy for real-field deployment on cluttered backgrounds.

Solution: TinyVarNet is a 28K-parameter dispersion-gated feedforward network with parameter-free dynamic routing that provides intrinsic multi-scale spatial explainability. It achieves 82.03% accuracy on highly cluttered real-field benchmarks while remaining small enough for edge hardware deployment.

Knowledge Distillation for Edge Vision Transformers

Problem: State-of-the-art Vision Transformers with 300M+ parameters are computationally infeasible for edge deployment in smart agriculture, yet smaller models lack the representational capacity for accurate disease classification.

Solution: LiteViT implements a cross-architecture knowledge distillation pipeline that compresses a 300M-parameter Vision Transformer into a 3.8 MB MobileViT-XXS. The distilled model achieves 99.3% accuracy at just 12.26 ms inference latency on edge hardware.

Equivariant Networks for Shortcut-Aware Recognition

Problem: Standard convolutional networks exploit background texture shortcuts when classifying crops and weeds, leading to brittle models that fail under orientation and viewpoint changes in the field.

Solution: E2-WeedNet is a sub-60K parameter C₈ group-equivariant convolutional network equipped with a parameter-free Group-CAM operator. This design explicitly mitigates background shortcut learning and improves orientation-robust weed recognition for practical field deployments.

Future Directions

Smart Agriculture: Hyper-Localized AI Models

My long-term vision centers on building hyper-localized AI models for agriculture that respect the incredible diversity of intercropping systems and agro-practices across different regions and environments. Rather than one-size-fits-all solutions, I envision a future where lightweight, privacy-preserving foundation models can be rapidly adapted to local crop varieties, soil conditions, and farming practices — deployed directly on edge devices in the field, empowering farmers with real-time, trustworthy decision support without requiring cloud connectivity.
